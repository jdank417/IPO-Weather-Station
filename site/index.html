How these odds work, and what they aren't
  • Every trading day we fit a standard volatility model (Heston) to Cboe's VIX indices (expected turbulence from 9 days to 6 months), VVIX (how jumpy that turbulence is) and the S&P 500's recent behavior. The model then prices every possible move.
  • These are option-market odds: what traders pay to insure against, or bet on, a move. They include an insurance premium, so historically they have overstated how often big drops actually happen.
  • The model has no sudden "jump" crashes, so it probably understates the chance of extreme one-day collapses.
  • Moves are measured from the latest close and include the small expected drift from interest rates minus dividends. Not investment advice.
